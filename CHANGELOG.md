## 18 November 2026

**Hypernetwork that writes a LoRA per question, trained through Qwen's loss** (`harness/hyper.py`; plan and review in `results/proposals/hypernetwork_ablation.md`). The frozen Qwen3.5-0.8B is run on the question; the hypernetwork reads the activations going into each of the 24 layers [24, N, 1024], pools them with 8 learned queries to [24, 8, 512], and a per-layer head (512 → 4,608) writes one row of A [8, 3584] and one column of B [1024, 8] per query: a rank-8 LoRA on every `mlp.down_proj`; 59.25M trainable parameters. Training: cross-entropy of one of Qwen's own correct, finished answers with the LoRA applied, backpropagated through frozen Qwen into the hypernetwork. Controls: mode `static` (the pooled vectors are a learned constant: a fixed LoRA of the same shape, same data and steps) and scoring with another question's activations (`junk`). Scoring generates with one LoRA per question in PyTorch (same sampling settings as the harness; unmodified Qwen is scored through the same path as the reference). Smoke test (`harness.hyper::smoke`): shapes as above; the LoRA starts as no change (loss 0.4146 with and without); gradients reach the head, queries and projection; 24 steps on 6 answers lower each answer's loss (e.g. 0.415 → 0.347, 0.173 → 0.122); 3.6 GB peak, about 1.6 s per training example. Two fixes found by the smoke test: the LoRA must stay applied until backward() because the checkpointed layers are recomputed then; the pooled vectors are normalised and scaled by 1/512 before the head, so an optimizer step moves a LoRA entry by about the learning rate (without it the loss went 0.41 → 30 in two steps). Training answers are being generated (`harness.hyper::answers`: practice problems outside select and confirm, 4 samples each, 6,000-token cap).

**Gradient request rebuilt for arm C: full weight gradients, gradient edits allowed** (`--grad-tool`; `harness/target.py::_gradient`, `rl/loop.py::with_grad_text`). The request now saves G = d(target)/dW for all 318 tensors of the transformer blocks to `/work/fwd_<k>/grads.safetensors` (0.93 GiB, bfloat16), the top singular pair (u, v) of every 2-D gradient to `rank1.safetensors`, and per-tensor norms, sigma1, rank-1 share and sum(G·W) to `gradient.json`. Targets: `logprob` or `logit` of given tokens at given positions, or `loss`. Up to 8 texts of 8,192 tokens: the output head runs only on the chosen positions and the layers are checkpointed. The request never changes the weights. In this arm the prompt no longer forbids gradients ("Gradients are disabled", RULES (a), guide item 11 are replaced) and says the editor may build edits from gradients: a gradient step, several steps, a rank-1, masked or projected version, or a hand-built edit. Both arms' full prompts were rendered and diffed before launch; they differ only in the gradient statements.

**Texts from files, for both arms.** Teacher-forced and gradient requests accept `"texts_file": "/work/<name>.json"` (a JSON list of raw texts or of chats), so the editor can pass the model's saved answers without pasting them. Tool results are cut at 10,000 characters, as the prompt states (was 6,000).

**Proposed: SFT route for reading and writing weights** (`results/proposals/next_experiments.md`, experiment 9). Not run.

**Gradient tool and its ablation** (`--grad-tool`; `results/eval/grad_tool_ablation.md`). A forward request type `gradient`: one backward pass on up to 8 texts, returning activation × gradient attributions of a target (log-prob of given tokens, or the loss) per layer, MLP unit and attention head; no optimizer, weights unchanged (smoke test: `harness.target --step gradsmoke`). Ablation with the step-8 editor on the starting model `cur4g16-s8best`, 4 sessions per arm: current tools reward +1.2 / MATH-500 −1.2 per session; with the gradient tool +0.4 / +1.4 (3 of 4 sessions submitted no edit). The tool was used in 1 of 4 sessions (3 calls: one on a mistyped text, one out of memory at ~4,000 tokens). Neither arm finds a gain beyond the loop fix. Two limits found: forward requests take texts as literal strings (the editor cannot reference its saved answers), and texts near 4,096 tokens run out of memory next to the packed vLLM servers.
